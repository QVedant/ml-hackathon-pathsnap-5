The data files

Each trip is a folder <id>/ with four whitespace-separated files without headers. Each trip comes with its own road network.

FileColumnsMeaning
.tracklon, lat, timestamp (s)Noisy GPS fixes of the vehicle. ~1 Hz (94% of steps are exactly 1 s). This is the input.
.nodeslon, latRoad-network junctions / shape points. Row number = node id (0-based).
.arcsfrom_node, to_nodeDirected road segments between two nodes. Row number = arc id. A two-way street is two arcs (A→B and B→A); about 95% of arcs have a reverse twin.
.routearc_idGround truth: the ordered list of arcs the vehicle drove. No timestamps, so one arc covers many GPS points.
  • Partitions: 80 train, 10 student-test, 10 evaluator (held by organisers, never used in development).
  • Train: 208,417 GPS points; networks range from 649 to 307,392 arcs. Student-test: 19,160 points.
  • Coordinates are around Kyoto, Japan (≈135.7°E, 34.9°N).
  • A GPS row does not correspond to a route row, so we had to create per-point labels (see preprocessing).

Preprocessing and EDA Venkatesh

  • Checks: 0 missing values, 0 duplicate timestamps, 0 out-of-order timestamps in all 90 trips. 1,216 gaps longer than 5 s in train. All route arc ids exist in the arc file.
  • Cleaning: sort by time, drop NaN or duplicate timestamps, drop points more than ~1 km outside the network bounding box.
  • Projection: lon/lat → UTM zone 53N (EPSG:32653), so every distance is in metres. Degrees are not distances: at 35°N one degree of longitude ≈ 91 km, one degree of latitude ≈ 111 km.
  • Smoothing: rolling median over 5 points, used only to compute heading and speed. Distances use the raw position.
  • Candidate generation: sample points every 15 m along every arc, put them in a KD-tree, find arcs near each GPS point, compute the exact point-to-segment distance, keep the K = 8 nearest arcs within R = 50 m.
  • Labels: dynamic-programming alignment of the GPS points to the ordered route. It may only move forward along the route (max 20 arcs per step) and minimises the total distance. A greedy pointer we tried first got lost on trip 8 (miss rate 41% → 2% after the fix).
  • Bad trips: in trips 33 and 54 the GPS lies about 150 m from their own reference route for the whole trip, so their labels are wrong. Excluded from training (rule: median GPS-to-route distance > 50 m).
  • Candidate miss rate (true arc not among the 8 candidates): 2.0% train, 0.45% student-test. That is the ceiling on accuracy.
  • EDA network check (networkx): largest weakly connected component holds 97% of nodes on average; no isolated nodes.

Features Vedant Sharma

Framing: one row per (GPS point, candidate arc), binary label “is this the true arc?”. Arc ids are different in every trip, so they cannot be classes; per-candidate features work across trips. 1,023,030 train rows, 17% positive.

13 features engineered (all new, computed from geometry; LightGBM split-count importance in brackets, highest first):

FeatureWhyRank
arc_lengthshort arcs at junctions are easy to confuse1
heading_diffGPS bearing vs arc bearing (0–180°). Separates parallel roads and the two directions of a two-way road2
proj_fracwhere along the arc the point projects (0–1)3
speedat low speed the heading is unreliable4
distperpendicular distance to the arc in metres: the main noise signal5
dist_gap_nearesthow much farther than the nearest candidate6
in_deg, out_degjunction complexity around the arc7–8
dist_rank0 = nearest candidate; relative closeness matters in dense areas9
n_candnumber of arcs within 50 m: how ambiguous the area is10
conn_prev_nearestarc continues from the previous point's nearest arc: cheap sequential context11
off_endpoint projects beyond the end of the arc, so it probably belongs to the next one12
dtseconds since the previous fix; after a gap the position is less reliable13

Dropped / not used as features (6 columns): raw lon, lat and timestamp t (they describe where/when the trip is, not whether an arc matches, and would not generalise to new trips), trip, point and arc ids (identifiers, leak trip identity). label and true_arc are targets. We did not drop any of the 13 engineered features after training.

Scaling: StandardScaler fitted on train only, used for LogReg, KNN and MLP. Trees don't need it.

How we prevented overfitting

  • GroupKFold by trip: all points of a trip stay in one fold. Consecutive GPS points are almost identical, so random splitting would leak.
  • Separate student-test trips for comparison; evaluator trips never touched.
  • Per model: LogReg L2 (C=1); KNN large k = 25; RF max_depth 16 and min_samples_leaf 5; LightGBM row/column subsampling 0.8 and small learning rate; MLP L2 alpha and early stopping on a validation split.
  • Evidence: CV and test macro-F1 differ by ≤ 0.008 for every model and fold std is ≤ 0.009. No model overfits. LogReg is slightly underfit (lowest scores: it can't learn interactions like “close but wrong direction”).
  • Class imbalance (17% positive) handled with class weights, not resampling.

Final pick: LightGBM Vaibhav

  • Primary metric: student-test Top-1. The route is built from the top candidate at every point, so that's what matters. F1 at a 0.5 threshold doesn't measure it.
  • LightGBM has the best Top-1 (0.875). Its log loss (0.152) is close to the best, so its probabilities are good enough as HMM emissions.
  • Trains 10× faster than Random Forest (7 s vs 77 s) and is 3 MB instead of 195 MB, which suits the API and Bayesian tuning.
  • MLP is the runner-up: best log loss and F1, but 0.4 points lower Top-1.
  • KNN rejected: worst log loss (0.32, coarse probabilities) and ~60× slower inference than LightGBM.
  • Training accuracy was not used to choose: all models are within ~1% of each other, so we used held-out trips.

Bayesian hyperparameter tuning Vaibhav

  • Method: Optuna with the TPE sampler (Tree-structured Parzen Estimator). TPE models which parameter values gave good scores and which gave bad ones, and samples where the ratio good/bad is highest. Each trial learns from earlier ones. Grid and random search do not.
  • First 10 trials are random exploration (TPE's startup), the rest are guided. Seed 42. Trial 0 is the untuned configuration, for a fair comparison.
  • Objective: maximise mean observation-level Top-1 over 3-fold GroupKFold on the 78 clean train trips. Student-test is used only to compare tuned vs untuned afterwards.

HMM and Viterbi Venkatesh

The classifier scores each point independently, so consecutive predictions can jump between unconnected roads. The HMM chooses the most likely connected sequence.

  • Hidden states: the candidate arcs of each GPS point (up to 8 per point).
  • Observations: the GPS points.
  • Emission: log P(arc is correct) from LightGBM.
  • Transition: compares the driving distance between the two candidates (Dijkstra shortest path on the directed network, up to 1.5 km) with the straight-line GPS distance. If the vehicle really drove that arc pair, the two distances are similar. Unreachable pairs get probability 0.
log A(i→j) = −| d_network(i, j) − d_gps(t−1, t) | / β (β multiplied by the time gap dt) δ_t(j) = max_i [ δ_{t−1}(i) + log A(i→j) ] + log B_t(j) then backtrack the stored argmax pointers from the best final state
  • Gaps: β grows with dt, so after a long gap larger detours are allowed.
  • Disconnected roads / no valid transition: the chain breaks and Viterbi restarts from that point (0.6 breaks per trip on average).
  • Points without candidates are skipped.
  • β chosen on student-test (swept 2, 5, 10, 20, 50); β = 50 gave the best Top-1.

API and UI Vedant Sharma (API) · Vedant Srivastava (UI)

  • Laptop 1: uv run uvicorn api.main:app --host 0.0.0.0 --port 8000. --host 0.0.0.0 makes it reachable from the other laptop. The evaluator trips folder is set with PATHSNAP_EVAL_DIR.
  • Endpoints: GET /health, GET /trips (lists trip ids), POST /predict {"trip_id": "..."}.
  • /predict pipeline: load track, nodes, arcs (not the route) → clean → candidates → 13 features → LightGBM probabilities → Viterbi → predicted arcs. Then the server reads the reference route and computes accuracy. It returns GPS points, the predicted route geometry and the metrics. The reference route never leaves the server.
  • Logs: every request prints the trip id, classifier and HMM Top-1, continuity, route F1 and time. Keep this terminal visible for the examiner.
  • Laptop 2: cd ui && python3 -m http.server 5500, open http://localhost:5500/?api=http://<laptop1-ip>:8000. The UI lists the trips, sends the chosen id, draws GPS (blue), classifier-only (orange) and HMM route (red) on a Leaflet map with a metrics table.
  • CORS is enabled so the browser on laptop 2 may call laptop 1. Leaflet is bundled locally, so the UI works without internet (map tiles need internet; the route still draws without them).
  • Typical time per trip: 1–5 s.

Rapid-fire Q&A

What is a .track file? What is an .arc?

.track = GPS fixes: longitude, latitude, timestamp in seconds, ~1 per second. .arcs = directed road segments, each a pair (from_node, to_node) indexing rows of .nodes; the row number is the arc id that .route refers to.

How many features did you make? How many dropped?

13 engineered per (point, candidate). None of the 13 dropped after training. Not used as features: raw lon, lat, timestamp, and trip/point/arc ids (6 columns), because they identify the trip rather than describe the match.

Most important feature?

By LightGBM split count: arc_length, then heading_diff, proj_frac, speed, dist. Split count favours continuous features; distance and heading are what physically separate the right road from its neighbours.

How many hidden layers in the MLP?

Two: 64 and 32 neurons, ReLU, Adam, early stopping. It stopped after 35 epochs.

Random Forest depth? Number of trees?

max_depth 16, 200 trees, min_samples_leaf 5, √(13) ≈ 3 features per split.

LightGBM parameters?

Untuned: 400 trees, lr 0.05, 63 leaves, subsample 0.8, colsample 0.8. Tuned (final): 550 trees, lr 0.032, 229 leaves, min_child_samples 59, subsample 0.60, colsample 0.52, reg_lambda 0.68.

How many tuning trials? Did tuning improve the model?

40 TPE trials, 17 minutes. Small improvement in CV Top-1 (0.8836 → 0.8844) and log loss (−4%); student-test Top-1 essentially unchanged. We report this honestly: the defaults were already good.

Value of k in KNN and why?

k = 25 with distance weighting: smooths noisy labels and gives less jumpy probabilities. Trained on 150k rows because predicting means comparing with every stored row.

Why GroupKFold and not KFold?

Points within a trip are strongly correlated. KFold would put neighbouring points in train and validation, inflating scores.

Why Top-1 and not accuracy as main metric?

Row accuracy is inflated by the many easy negatives (83% of rows). Top-1 measures what we need: picking the right road for every GPS point.

Why UTM?

We need distances in metres. In degrees, a step east and a step north of the same size are different distances at 35°N.

Why is this classification and not regression?

We decide for each candidate road whether it is the true one. The probabilities rank candidates and feed the HMM.

Why Bayesian and not grid search?

Each trial takes ~30 s. TPE uses earlier results to choose the next point, so it finds good regions in fewer trials than grid or random.

How do you handle class imbalance?

17% positives. class_weight balanced (LogReg, RF), is_unbalance (LightGBM). Top-1 per point is insensitive to the threshold anyway.

Limitations?

Labels come from aligning GPS to an untimed route, so they are approximate near junctions. Two trips had GPS far from the route. Arcs are treated as straight lines. 2% of train points have no correct candidate.

Is the ground truth used in prediction?

No. The API loads the route only after prediction to compute accuracy (load_trip_dir sets route to None and predict_trip asserts it).
